Snow White Waffle Plant

Strobilanthes alternata ‘Snow White’

The variegated waffle plant — crinkled, quilted leaves of light green splashed with white and cream, edged in pink, over a vivid magenta-purple underside. Raised as a seedling of the purple ‘Exotica’ waffle plant and selected for its heavy white variegation.

Leaves sit in opposite pairs — one of each pair noticeably larger — finely hairy, 2–3 inches long, with toothed, wavy edges and sunken veins. Stems are purplish, darkest at the nodes, and root where they touch soil. In summer it can make small white bell flowers penciled in purple, opening from the leaf joints.

🌿 The name: Hemigraphis is Greek for “half brush,” for the half-hairy stamens. In its homeland the species is Murikooti, an Ayurvedic wound herb — “Vranaropani,” the wound healer.

🎛️ Conditions — Measured

A forest-floor plant from the humid undergrowth of Malaysia and Java — it wants the light that filters through a canopy, not a windowsill bake. Direct sun bleaches and scorches the white variegation; too little light and the leaves give up their color and go plain green.

📋 The Care Card

LightBright indirect or dappled shade — a north or east window, or a few feet back from south/west. 6–8 hours a day keeps the white bright.
WaterKeep evenly moist but never soggy — roughly weekly, more in heat. Water when the top half-inch dries. It wilts hard when thirsty; don’t let it reach the wilt. Never leave it standing in water — roots rot.
HumidityMist 2–3 times a week, or use a pebble tray, humidifier, or terrarium. Dry indoor air crisps the pink edges.
SoilLight, rich, well-draining mix that still holds moisture. Pot must have drainage holes.
FeedingLight balanced feed monthly, spring through early fall.
PruningPinch stem tips for a bushy mound; cut leggy stems back at the base. Repot when roots show at the drainage holes.

✂️ Propagation — It Wants to Multiply

  1. Late spring to mid-summer, snip a healthy stem 3–4 inches long, just below a node. Stems near the soil often already carry starter roots at the nodes.
  2. Strip the lower leaves.
  3. Root in water, or straight into moist, rich soil — both work; rooting hormone is optional.
  4. Keep warm and humid until it takes hold, then pot on.

🩺 Troubleshooting

Leaves going plain greenToo little light — move brighter (still indirect).
Bleached, scorched patchesDirect sun — pull it back from the window.
Crispy leaf edgesAir too dry — mist, tray, or terrarium.
Sudden total wiltThirst — water deeply; it usually recovers within hours.
Fine webbing, stippled leavesSpider mites (its main pest, with aphids and whitefly) — rinse and treat.

Dragon’s TongueH. repanda — narrow, rippled purple leaves

Fast-growing and low — 6–8 inches tall, spreading 12–24 inches and rooting as it goes. It cascades from a hanging basket, carpets a terrarium, or spills over a pot rim exactly the way yours is starting to do.
