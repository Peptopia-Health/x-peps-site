McCoy Floraline Pedestal Planter

The tall ribbed pedestal pot on the right of the windowsill — what it is, and how to prove it.

Maker — McCoy Pottery (probable)Line — FloralineEra — 1960s

The identification: the tall pedestal form, fine vertical ribbing, and matte earth-tone glaze match McCoy Pottery's Floraline line — the ware McCoy of Roseville, Ohio made for the florist trade through the 1960s. Labeled probable, not certain: the same factory made thousands of near-twin pedestals, and the deciding evidence is the molded mark on the base, which has not been photographed yet.

The plant in it is an elephant bush (Portulacaria afra), top-dressed with river stones — exactly the kind of planting these florist pedestals were sold for.

Why It Points to Floraline

Pedestal compote form

A flared, footed cup on a trumpet base — Floraline's signature florist shape, made to lift an arrangement to table height.

Vertical ribbing

Fine, even fluting runs the full height of the cup. Ribbed pedestals are among the most-listed Floraline molds (407, 424, 430, 685).

Matte earth-tone glaze

The flat brown-olive finish matches Floraline's matte palette — avocado, olive, moss, and brown glazes sold to shops, not showrooms.

Florist scale

A hand-span cup on a tall foot: sized for a florist's arrangement or a small houseplant, not a floor planter.

The Rest of the Family

Documented Floraline pedestal molds include the 407 and 430 ribbed pedestal vases, the 424 footed ribbed planter in brown, the 463 / 464 matte olive and brown pedestals, and the 685 ribbed melon planter — all 1960s, all Roseville, Ohio.

Pin the Exact Year — Check the Base

Floraline pieces are marked in the mold on the underside: "Floraline" + the mold number + "USA," and many carry the year itself — a documented example reads "Floraline 1968 USA." One photo of the bottom turns this page's "probable" into a fact, mold number and all.

Value

Comparable Floraline pedestal planters list and sell in the $18–$50 range, by size, glaze, and condition — modest money, real mid-century American pottery. Sources: current listings for the matte green pedestal, the green swirl pedestal, and the Floraline market overall.
