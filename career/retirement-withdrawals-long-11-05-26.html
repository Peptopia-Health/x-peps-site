How to Read These Tables

The method, in the open

Every number in the two scenario tables is built the same transparent way:

  • Total income is the standing on-file projection for that retirement age — the same totals your briefings carry ($118,830/yr at 62 · $164,908/yr at 67).
  • FERS pension is the saved component split from your confirmed service record — an estimate, contingent on the pending HR verification.
  • Social Security is the official Nov 5, 2026 statement amount for that start age.
  • Portfolio withdrawal is what is left: total minus pension minus Social Security. It is a balancing figure from the saved model — not a new projection.
  • Amounts are held level in today's dollars for every year. No market growth, no cost-of-living increases, and no new assumptions were added to build these tables.
  • The first rows of each table are bridge years — still working, living on VA pay — so you can see the handoff year where the three retirement streams switch on.

Watch Items

What could move these numbers

1 · Pension verification — pending

Every pension figure here assumes the full 9.2 creditable years (7.2 CIA FERS + ~2 Air Force bought back). HR verification with Lara Glory is still pending. If only 7.2 years verify, pension — and therefore every scenario total — drops $3,335/yr. This is the single most likely near-term change to this guide.

2 · Social Security's earnings assumption

The official statement's estimates assume continued earnings of $30,100/yr until benefits start. That is SSA's stated basis, printed on the statement itself — your actual VA earnings run higher, and future statements will restate the ladder on your real record.

3 · Balances are dated Nov 2, 2026

The $1,236,309 total is the Nov 2, 2026 re-baseline. Markets move it daily; a full re-baseline with fresh TSP and fuller VA-pay history is due in late November 2026. The portfolio withdrawal in the tables is the saved model's balancing figure — when the re-baseline lands, this guide gets rebuilt from it.

4 · Concentration

At the Oct 25, 2026 assessment, Chevron was about 22% of everything you own, and Chevron + Apple together about 34%. A single-company event is a larger risk to this plan than your spending is.

5 · TSP is 100% stocks

Your TSP sits at 61% S Fund / 39% I Fund — no stabilizer. That is a growth engine while you work; sequence risk grows as a retirement date gets close, and it is worth a deliberate look before either finish line.

6 · Cards stay at $0

The need figures assume no card interest — true today, with the Fidelity Visa at $0. Any month interest reappears, the live-as-now need creeps up; keeping cards at zero is the cheapest raise in this whole plan.
